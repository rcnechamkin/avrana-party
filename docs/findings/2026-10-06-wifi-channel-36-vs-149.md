# Wi-Fi findings: 2026-10-06 (one-tablet baseline, home test)

Historical evidence of what was measured on 2026-10-06, not a contract. Raw runs are in
`tools/wifi-lab/results/` (gitignored: MACs, IPs, device names). Harness and rerun commands:
[wifi-lab README](../../tools/wifi-lab/README.md). The current network policy this led to is in
[network runbook](../runbooks/network.md) (Channel policy) and the procedures in
[wifi-qualification](../runbooks/wifi-qualification.md); both were written after this finding and may differ from it.

## Setup

- Pi 4 (`party`), built-in `wlan0` (brcmfmac, BCM43455, firmware 7.45.265) as the AP, NetworkManager profile
  `Avrana Party Internal`, 10.42.0.0/24 (`ipv4.method shared`), country US.
- One Samsung SM-T510 tablet (Android 11, 32-bit armv7) about arm's length from the Pi, controlled over USB/adb.
  Route to the AP verified to leave via the tablet's `wlan0`; no other interface held an IPv4 address.
- Original AP configuration: **5 GHz, channel 149 (5745 MHz), 20 MHz**. Not 2.4 GHz, as had been assumed.
  Backup: `tools/wifi-lab/results/_bootstrap/pi-ap-config-backup.txt` (local only).
- Tests: 400 pings at 200 ms from the tablet to 10.42.0.1 (the Android non-root minimum interval), plus a
  home-made load generator (paced UDP and `nc` TCP; not iperf3, see README limits). Throughput is the
  tablet's own `wlan0` byte counters.

## Findings

### 1. The Pi firmware mailbox was wedged before the reboot (separate problem)

Since boot (20:06): `Firmware transaction 0x00030046 timeout`, `hwmon: Failed to get throttled (-110)`,
v3d clock enable failure, audio `vchi message timeout`. `vcgencmd get_throttled` hung in uninterruptible
sleep (4 stuck processes inflated the load average to ~6 on an idle-ish Pi).

- Consequence 1: **under-voltage/throttle state was unobservable**, so a power problem could not be excluded.
- Consequence 2: downlink delivered only ~1.7 Mbps even when 5–20 Mbps was offered, with RTT of 0.5–1.4 s.
  After the reboot the same test delivered the offered rate up to ~47 Mbps. The collapse was this fault
  (or something it caused), not RF.
- After reboot: `throttled=0x0`, load 0.1, temperature 34–37 °C, no stuck processes. Not reproduced since.
  Cause unknown; it is a candidate for a recurring failure (see the existing power-undervoltage notes).

### 2. Channel 149 was bad; channel 36 was good (same Pi, tablet, distance)

All figures are the 3 idle trials per channel after the reboot unless noted. Times in ms.

| Metric | ch149 | ch36 |
|---|---|---|
| p50 RTT | 10.4–11.0 | 5.8–6.0 |
| p95 | 56–65 | 36–39 |
| p99 | 140–211 | 64–99 |
| max | 213–313 | 105–188 |
| packet loss | 0–0.25% | 0% |
| tablet RSSI (dBm) | −52 to −54 | −42 to −45 |
| tablet TX retries per success | 30–36 | 0.6–0.7 |
| negotiated TX rate | 86 Mbps | 86 Mbps |
| Pi-reported TX failures | 0 | 0 |

Loaded (30 s per level, ch149 → ch36):

| Load | RTT p50 / p99, loss on ch149 | RTT p50 / p99, loss on ch36 |
|---|---|---|
| down 20 Mbps | 17 / 364, 0.7% | 5 / 150, 0% |
| down 30 Mbps | 23 / 1111, 1.3% | 10 / 179, 0% |
| down 50 Mbps | 2146 / 5339, 83% loss; delivered 47 | 49 / 220, 0.7%; delivered 49 |
| TCP down, max | 659 / 1644; 19 Mbps | 130 / 587; 46 Mbps |
| TCP up, max | 509 / 9927; **1.75 Mbps** | 394 / 997; **49 Mbps** |

Uplink (tablet→AP) was the badly broken direction on 149: ~1.75 Mbps and ~10 s RTT spikes versus ~49 Mbps on
36. Downlink degraded gradually above ~20 Mbps on 149.

### 3. It was not neighbouring Wi-Fi

A tablet-side scan (`cmd wifi list-scan-results`, 60 strongest networks) showed the crowding on channels 40
and 44 (5200/5220 MHz, Xfinity/hotspot gear). Channel 36 had nothing else on it, and channel 149 had nothing
else on it either; a few weak networks sit on ~157 (5785 MHz). So a "count the neighbours" scanner would have
rated 149 and 36 as equally clean, yet 149 performed an order of magnitude worse.

Likely explanations, **not tested** (speculation): non-Wi-Fi interference near 5745 MHz; a brcmfmac/antenna
or regulatory/tx-power issue on the UNII-3 segment of this particular Pi (the driver reports 31 dBm); or a
tablet front-end weakness on 149. A second client, or the same test with another Pi, would separate these.

### 4. Other observations

- **Power-save latency floor.** A 200 ms ping interval lets the tablet radio doze; with a 100 ms beacon
  interval and DTIM 2, some idle spikes (p95 ~40–60 ms) are partly power-save, not link loss. Idle p50 on
  36 was ~6 ms but under light load (5 Mbps) fell to ~2.5 ms.
- **Queueing under saturation.** Max-effort uplink on 36 still pushed RTT p50 to ~400 ms. That is buffer
  delay (bufferbloat) in the tablet/AP path, not radio loss. Avrana traffic is small, so this matters only
  if a game shares the link with a bulk transfer (e.g. asset download).
- **Pi-side counters are thin.** `iw station dump` on brcmfmac gives `tx failed` only (0 throughout, even on
  149 with a 30:1 tablet retry ratio). Retries come from the tablet (`cmd wifi status`) and are noisy
  (one run showed a ~80k jump), so use the ratio as an indicator.
- **Channel utilisation is unmeasurable**: `iw survey dump` is empty for brcmfmac in AP mode.
- **A harmless kernel message** `brcmf_vif_set_mgmt_ie: vndr ie set error -52` appeared at the channel switch.
  No other `brcmfmac` errors; power-save on the AP is enabled (default).
- **Other clients matter.** Before the reboot an iPhone and the laptop were associated, adding airtime
  noise. The post-reboot runs had the tablet alone.
- **Tablet roams away on AP restart.** When the AP dropped for the channel change the tablet joined the home
  Wi-Fi (a saved home network); a Wi-Fi off/on via adb brought it back. Real phones will do the same.
- **Regulatory**: US/DFS-FCC, channels 36–48 and 149–165 are non-DFS and permitted; 52–144 are DFS.

## State at the end of testing

- AP is on **channel 36** (the owner ran the change on the Pi with sudo). Original was 149; restore with
  `sudo nmcli connection modify "Avrana Party Internal" 802-11-wireless.band a 802-11-wireless.channel 149 &&
  sudo nmcli connection up "Avrana Party Internal"`. Repo docs (`docs/runbooks/network.md`, `docs/SYSTEM.md`)
  still describe 149.
- `nmcli` over SSH as `cody` is refused by polkit and passwordless sudo is not configured, so the harness
  cannot change the AP itself; its `ap set/restore` commands fail loudly.

## Speculation: how to improve the system

Ordered by value for "one bar-table night, phones join and play".

1. **Pick the channel by measurement, not by default.** Because a clean-looking channel (149) performed
   terribly, the choice should combine (a) a pre-AP station-mode scan to avoid crowded channels and
   (b) a real link test against a connected client, falling back through a short list of non-DFS candidates
   (36, 40, 44, 48, 149, 153, 157, 161) with 36 as the safe default. Do it at boot, before the AP comes up
   (brcmfmac cannot survey while serving an AP, and a switch disconnects everyone). Offer a Host-UI
   "re-optimise Wi-Fi" action between games only; never auto-switch mid-game. Log scan, scores and decision.
   Needs a root unit and owner approval (AGENTS.md reserves NetworkManager/Wi-Fi), so it belongs in an AVR issue.
2. **Make the Pi's health observable and self-reporting.** The wedged firmware mailbox hid power/throttle
   state and quietly wrecked throughput. Add a watchdog that alarms (Host UI, Beszel) on
   `Failed to get throttled`, `Firmware transaction ... timeout`, stuck `vcgencmd` processes, or
   `get_throttled != 0x0`, and read throttle via a bounded-time call. Revisit the power supply/cable: the
   mailbox timeouts and the earlier under-voltage history may be one story. Do this before any deeper RF tuning.
3. **Turn the harness into a pre-event gate.** A 2-minute `idle` + `down-20M` run on the actual venue channel
   with a real phone, pass/fail on p99 and retry ratio, shown in the Host UI or run from the laptop. The
   harness already writes machine-readable summaries; add thresholds to `config.json` and a `gate` command.
4. **Instrument the real workload.** Per-client RTT/jitter and reconnect counts measured by the game
   protocol itself (the Party session heartbeat) give data from actual phones in the actual room, which no
   synthetic test can. Correlate with Pi-side counters; then the harness's Gauntlet module has something to
   plug into (design in README).
5. **Second-client and second-Pi A/B.** To resolve finding 3: test a second Android device on 149 and 36, and
   if 149 is still bad, look at this board's antenna/tx-power. If a bar has bad 5 GHz everywhere, plan a
   2.4 GHz fallback channel (1/6/11) with a "Limited Mode" capacity warning.
6. **Reduce airtime pressure.** Keep game traffic small and bursty; avoid bulk transfers over the AP during
   play; consider disabling AP client isolation overhead only if not needed, and test brcmfmac power-save off
   (`802-11-wireless.powersave 2`) for lower idle latency at the cost of a little more power.
7. **Roaming/rejoin behaviour.** Document or handle the "phone falls back to home/cellular Wi-Fi when the AP
   restarts" case; a captive-style notice or the join QR on the host screen shortens recovery.
8. **Capacity testing.** The next scalability question is client count, not single-client speed: 6–10
   Android/iOS clients each sending small updates. That needs the multi-client harness extension listed in
   the README, and ideally 2–3 spare Android devices or emulated load from the laptop.
9. **Alternative hardware, only if measurements demand it.** A USB Wi-Fi adapter was tried and removed for
   power reasons. If the built-in radio stays marginal, a better AP (a small travel router in AP mode fed by
   the Pi over Ethernet) would give proper survey/ACS, better antennas and more clients, at the cost of
   another device in the bag. Don't decide this before items 1–2 and the second-client test.

## Confidence

High: ch36 >> ch149 on this setup; the firmware-fault cleared by reboot coincided with downlink recovery;
uplink was the broken direction on 149. Medium: the cause of 149's retries (not neighbours; mechanism untested).
Low/untested: behaviour with multiple clients, with phones (vs. the tablet), at another venue, over a full
event, and whether the firmware fault returns.
